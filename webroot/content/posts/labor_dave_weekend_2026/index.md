I've always wanted to see Dave Matthews Band live, so when dates for this summer's
tour were announced earlier this year, we decided to go to the Denver show.

And just before the on-sale date, we got the crazy idea to also buy tickets to see
them at The Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State. We'd drive our self-built
camper van out there and make a bit trip out of it. Well, it wasn't built *yet*,
but this would be the motivation we needed to finish the build.

Dave Matthews Band plays all three nights of Labor Day weekend at the Gorge Amphitheater
every year, something I learned that the fans call "Labor Dave Weekend."

*Note: If you say "The Gorge" to someone familiar with Washington, they may think
you're talking about the Columbia River Gorge. That's on the border of Washington
and Oregon. The Gorge Amphitheater is in central Washington in a town called George
(yes -- George, Washington). They're not anywhere near each other.*

## Day by day

### Monday August 31

We set out in the morning from Estes Park, stopping for lunch in
Laramie, Wyoming at a cleverly-named brewery called
[The Library](https://thelibrarybrewery.com/).

Our destination for the night was [Firehole Canyon
Campground](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/firehole-campground),
a Forest Service campground a little south of Rock Springs, Wyoming.  Some
Forest Service campgrounds are free, but this one was on the pricey side at
$33. But we'd read it had showers, so thought that would be nice. So we
reserved a site online a few days before the trip.

When we arrived, we found that there was no camp host and just one other site
occupied. There was indeed a shower inside each bathroom, but we quickly
decided we didn't want to use them. I guess things were fairly clean for being
unattended, but it wasn't what we had in mind.

Something we learned about Forest Service campgrounds on this trip is that you
can't really tell much from how much they charge. There are free campsites that
are fantastic, and there are sites like this one that are a bit underwhelming.

Still, it was a nice area and we had the place pretty much to ourselves, so
not too bad.

### Tuesday September 1

{{<thumb-fig

We made our way to [Craters of the Moon National Monument](https://www.nps.gov/crmo/index.htm)
in Idaho. This was a great example of what we had in mind when we built the van.
We'd probably never think of visiting this place if we were taking a trip in a car,
staying in hotels, because there's not really anything around. But with the van,
we could make it a destination for the night.

There's a campground just inside the park, and it's fantastic. It's like you're camping
on the surface of the moon. Actually, we learned that NASA trained some astronauts here
in preparation for walking on the moon. And then they went to the moon and figured out that
it's not really like this place, it just looks a bit similar.

This place is where we started to learn that the price of the campground doesn't
tell you that much about it. This campground was $15 a night, and was way better than
the previous night's $33 site.

We did some exploring of Craters of the Moon in the morning, since we arrived
pretty late in the day yesterday. Then we headed out.

We stopped in Sun Valley, Idaho (a ski town) for lunch. Michele discovered
[publicshowers.net](https://publicshowers.net) -- yes, that's a real thing. And
we learned that for $7, we could buy a shower at the local YMCA. It was worth
every penny!

Our destination for the day was Pettit Lake in the Sawtooths. There's a Forest
Service campground there, and we planned to spend two nights. That would give
us all day tomorrow to hike to Alice Lake. We'd been putting in some longer
driving days in order to have a full day of hiking.

Unfortunately, the weather didn't go quite as we had hoped.

### Thursday September 3

src="salmon_river.jpg"

We had planned a hike for this day, to Alice Lake. And we'd stay at the same
camp site again so we didn't need to drive today. But sadly we awoke to the
sound of rain.

We've got the gear for hiking in the rain, but that doesn't mean it's something
we want to do. And especially not while we're living in the van. There's not
really anywhere to spread things out to dry. So we called off our hiking plans
and decided to just spend the day driving closer to Washington.

That afternoon, we stopped in McCall, Idaho to figure out just where we would
go for the night. McCall was a really cute town, and reminded us of Lake Tahoe.
We pored over our maps at a local brewery and decided we would continue to
Riggins, Idaho. We stayed at a <abbr title="Bureau of Land Management">BLM</abbr>-managed 
area (which means it's free, but has no services) on the Salmon River called the
Shorts Bar Recreation Area. It rained all evening and overnight so we were
stuck inside the van. The photo you see is from the next morning.

### Friday September 4

Today we finally got a hike in, albeit a small one. We drove just a few miles
to hike the Rapid River Trail. It goes on for plenty of miles, but we didn't
have a lot of time so just hiked a couple of miles.

We drove to Lewiston, Idaho. It's right on the Washington border, across the
river from Clarkston, Washington. (There's a *lot* of Lewis and Clark stuff out
here.) And thanks to publicshowers.net again, we found a gas station with
showers and were able to get ourselves cleaned up.

For tonight's camping, we decided to make use of our new Harvest Hosts
membership.  Michele found a winery just outside of town that also served food
in their tasting room, and they let you stay overnight in their parking lot. It
sounded great to us, though we found that both the food and wine was just
average. They seemed to focus more on hosting events like wedding receptions,
and I think this was more of an events center masquerading as a winery. Still,
not a bad place for the night.

{{<thumb-fig

This was the day before the concert. We didn't want to go all the way to
George (where the concert is) because there's basically nothing there except
the amphitheater. So we drove to Richland, Washington. As it turned out,
Richland was hosting the [Tumbleweed Music Festival](https://tumbleweedfest.com/)
in a city park all weekend for free.

Carrying our Florida Gators camp chairs to the park along the Columbia
River, I heard someone shout "Go Gators!" as we passed by. Wherever you are, you
can always find another Gator.

After enjoying a bit of the free music fetsival, we decided we'd find a hotel
for the night. We already had a hotel lined up for the night after the concert,
but we wanted to do some laundry, and after the concert we wouldn't be getting
to our hotel until quite late. That was one way to rationalize a hotel.

We had dinner at a winery's tasting room, did our laundry back at the hotel,
and had a nice leisurely evening.

### Sunday September 6

It's concert day!

We arrived in George several hours before the concert to avoid traffic,
especially since this was the first time we'd ever been to this venue.
The Gorge Amphitheater is basically in the middle of nowhere. There's a
campground next to it, but we couldn't stay there because they would
only sell you the entire Labor Day Weekend and we just needed one night
(plus they wanted about $600 for camping for those three nights). We had
booked a hotel, but all hotel options are a bit of a drive. We'd be staying
in Ephrata, which was maybe 30-45 minutes away, after the show.

As luck would have it, one of the very few things near the amphitheater
was a winery. [Cave B Winery](https://caveb.com/) was right next door,
so we visited there first -- as did hundreds of other concertgoers. Turns out,
decades ago the owner of the winery also owned The Gorge Amphitheater, which is
why it's next door. But then it was sold to corporate owners, and today
is owned by Live Nation, because of course it is.

After visiting the winery we parked at The Gorge, but we were still several
hours early. But that's part of the experience. A lot of the fans just hang out
next to their vehicle, set up tables, have some drinks, play music, even
cook food, and have a good time until the show starts. So that's what we did.
With the van, we had a table, chairs, even a fridge already right there with us.

Something we didn't expect is that many fans had gifts. Especially when people
heard it was our first Gorge show ever (and only our second Dave Matthews Band
show), they would give us a little something. Maybe a sticker, or a keychain,
or a bracelet, or little figurine that somehow tied in to some song.

I'd heard that this happened at Taylor Swift concerts, but I didn't know it
happened for Dave Matthews. It definitely didn't happen at the Denver show, so
maybe it's just a thing at The Gorge. Actually, someone told us that Saturday
afternoon of Labor Day weekend at the Gorge at 4:30 PM on the lawn is the time
that people meet up and swap their trinkets.

The Gorge is a really amazing venue. There are seats up close and a general admission
lawn farther up. We had bought seats because we didn't want to have to go in
as soon as doors opened to get a good spot on the lawn and then sit there for hours.
But a downside of the seats is that you're so low that you can't see the river
behind the stage. So we first went up to the top of the lawn to see what the view
looks like from there, before finding our seats.

It had been so long since we bought the tickets that I didn't remember anything
about our seats, but Michele told me she thought we got really good seats. Turns out,
we did, and the show was fantastic. We were going to skip the opening act (actually,
there were two openers), but someone told us not to miss the second opener -- Jesse Welles.
That was a great tip, because he was amazing.

Dave Matthews Band played for probably two and a half hours or maybe a little
more.  [Here's the
setlist](https://www.setlist.fm/setlist/dave-matthews-band/2026/the-gorge-amphitheatre-george-wa-5b4a63b4.html)
if you're curious. I made the mistake of checking the setlist from the night
before and saw that they played Led Zeppelin's "Fool In The Rain" with Pearl
Jam's Mike McCready. I should not have looked at that.  But still, this set was
awesome.

It felt like we were among the DMB superfans at The Gorge. When we mentioned to
someone that it was our first time at The Gorge, someone sitting in the
row in front of us who overheard us just turned around and handed us some stickers.
There was a father who was there with his sons, the youngest of which was probably
10 years old and told us this was his 33rd time seeing Dave Matthews Band live.
We definitely felt like we were in the minority for just seeing one night -- most
folks we talked to were there for all three shows of the weekend.

After the show, we slowly made our way back out to the van, and then we
*very* slowly made our way out of the parking lot and on to our hotel for the
night in Ephrata. That's when we realized this was the first time we'd ever
driven the van at night and that its headlights were ridiculously dim. On high,
they were just "pretty dim," so we drove like that to the hotel and made a note
to replace the headlights after we got home.

We were glad to have spent the previous night in a hotel and gotten our laundry
done, because we arrived after midnight and needed to get up fairly early
to hit the road the next day, so there wouldn't have been any time for laundry here.

Back in our working days (that's fun to say), for a while Michele worked for
someone who lived in Spokane, so we met up with him for lunch in Couer d'Alene, Idaho. It's
just over the Idaho border and really close to Spokane.

We were staying at a campground in Couer d'Alene for the night, and so we
planned to just spend the day there. It's a nice little town on a big lake with
lots of outdoor activities. Except that it rained pretty much all day. So we
visited a few shops in between rain showers.

As luck would have it, we found [a cocktail
bar](https://www.lacondesacda.com/) that was fantastic. One of those places
where they don't serve food, don't have TVs, and they don't let people stand at
the bar. Really nice.

When we got to the campground, it started raining a bit harder. So it was
another one of those nights where you just close everything up and you're in
the van for the night at 6PM.

In the morning, we took advantage of the shower at the campground (I think this makes
for three days in a row of showers!) and hit the road. We didn't bother to make
breakfast in the van so we could save time. Really it's not the breakfast that
takes so long, but making coffee. And really it's not making the coffee but
cleaning the things used to make the coffee. So we stopped on the road at one
of those drive-through coffee huts. I want to mention this one specifically --
[Basecamp Coffee](https://www.basecampcoffeekingston.com/) in Kingston, Idaho
-- because their breakfast burrito was fantastic!

Later in our drive, the van's check engine light came on after we hit a bump
in the road. Our van is pretty old (it's a 2010), but it's low miles, and a particularly
reliable year and engine, which went into our decision to buy this particular
van. So we were a little concerned. We were an hour-ish from Missoula, Montana,
and Michele was able to call around and find a mechanic who could fit us in.

Amazingly, Shane at Honesty Auto in Missoula had us back on the road probably an hour
after we arrived. The connection on the oxygen sensor had come loose, that's all.
I didn't know how serious the issue was, but Shane gave us a tip -- usually if the
check engine light isn't flashing, and you don't have any driveability problems,
you're good to drive and get it checked out later.




## Things we learned

Idaho is quite beautiful. I guess I was just expecting potatoes. (They definitely
have potatoes, but they also have lots of other things.)

We planned more days than we needed to get to Washington, and could have used
one or two more days for the trip back. We had to pick our trip dates pretty far in
advance in order to arrange a pet and house sitter, but we should have
done a little route planning before settling on them.

For us, the ideal time for a hotel night would be after three or maybe four
nights in the van. That would keep us from getting worn out from the van routine.
