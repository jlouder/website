I've always wanted to see Dave Matthews Band live, so when dates for this summer's
tour were announced earlier this year, we decided to go to the Denver show.

And just before the on-sale date, we got the crazy idea to also buy tickets to see
them at The Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State. We'd drive our self-built
camper van out there and make a bit trip out of it. Well, it wasn't built *yet*,
but this would be the motivation we needed to finish the build.

Dave Matthews Band plays all three nights of Labor Day weekend at the Gorge Amphitheater
every year, something I learned that the fans call "Labor Dave Weekend."

*Note: If you say "The Gorge" to someone familiar with Washington, they may think
you're talking about the Columbia River Gorge. That's on the border of Washington
and Oregon. The Gorge Amphitheater is in central Washington in a town called George
(yes -- George, Washington). They're not anywhere near each other.*

## Monday August 31

We set out in the morning from Estes Park, stopping for lunch in
Laramie, Wyoming at a cleverly-named brewery called
[The Library](https://thelibrarybrewery.com/).

Our destination for the night was [Firehole Canyon
Campground](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/firehole-campground),
a Forest Service campground a little south of Rock Springs, Wyoming.  Some
Forest Service campgrounds are free, but this one was on the pricey side at
$33. But we'd read it had showers, so thought that would be nice. So we
reserved a site online a few days before the trip.

When we arrived, we found that there was no camp host and just one other site
occupied. There was indeed a shower inside each bathroom, but we quickly
decided we didn't want to use them. I guess things were fairly clean for being
unattended, but it wasn't what we had in mind.

Something we learned about Forest Service campgrounds on this trip is that you
can't really tell much from how much they charge. There are free campsites that
are fantastic, and there are sites like this one that are a bit underwhelming.

Still, it was a nice area and we had the place pretty much to ourselves, so
not too bad.

We made our way to [Craters of the Moon National Monument](https://www.nps.gov/crmo/index.htm)
in Idaho. This was a great example of what we had in mind when we built the van.
We'd probably never think of visiting this place if we were taking a trip in a car,
staying in hotels, because there's not really anything around. But with the van,
we could make it a destination for the night.

There's a campground just inside the park, and it's fantastic. It's like you're camping
on the surface of the moon. Actually, we learned that NASA trained some astronauts here
in preparation for walking on the moon. And then they went to the moon and figured out that
it's not really like this place, it just looks a bit similar.

We did some exploring of Craters of the Moon in the morning, since we arrived
pretty late in the day yesterday. Then we headed out.

We stopped in Sun Valley, Idaho (a ski town) for lunch. Michele discovered
[publicshowers.net](https://publicshowers.net) -- yes, that's a real thing. And
we learned that for $7, we could buy a shower at the local YMCA. It was worth
every penny!

Our destination for the day was Pettit Lake in the Sawtooths. There's a Forest
Service campground there, and we planned to spend two nights. That would give
us all day tomorrow to hike to Alice Lake. We'd been putting in some longer
driving days in order to have a full day of hiking.

Unfortunately, the weather didn't go quite as we had hoped.
